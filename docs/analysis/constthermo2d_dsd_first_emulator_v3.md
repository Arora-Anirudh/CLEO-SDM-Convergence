# DSD-first emulator test for the 2-D CLEO ensemble

**Exploratory Mac-only readout, 1 October 2026. Decision: no-go for synthetic-member augmentation or higher-resolution convergence claims.** This test follows the [first emulator feasibility study](constthermo2d_stochastic_emulator_exploratory_v1.md) and its [whole-member-residual calibration follow-up](constthermo2d_stochastic_emulator_calibration_v2.md). No CLEO or Levante job was run.

## The specific problem tested

The earlier emulator predicted mass-weighted \(r_{90}\) as a smooth diagnostic independent of a coarse 25-bin mass DSD. In the actual convergence analysis, \(r_{90}\) is the geometric midpoint of the first radius bin where the **1,000-bin cumulative mass distribution** reaches 90%. A smooth predicted radius and an independently generated coarse DSD need not agree, even if their averages look reasonable. The present test generates a 1,000-bin mass DSD first and then applies the exact stored \(r_{90}\) rule. The script verifies that applying that rule to every one of the 120 real cached members reproduces its saved \(r_{90}\) to numerical precision before any fitting.

The cached experiment has 20 independent CLEO members at each of six resolutions, 128–4,096 initial superdroplets per populated cell, 61 saved times over 120 minutes, and a common 1,000-bin radius grid. The separate 8,192-cell-resolution cohort is *not* used as a clean high-resolution target because its initial-radius support differs from the lower-resolution cohorts. A sample generated by the emulator is not an independent CLEO realization.

## Model and tests

For each member and saved time, the mass DSD is converted to per-bin mass fractions and then a cumulative mass distribution (CDF). A 48-component PCA represents the full member-by-time 1,000-bin CDF. A small conditional neural network maps resolution to the mean PCA scores. To generate a complete synthetic trajectory, the method adds one resampled **whole-member CDF residual** from the nearest training resolution to this mean. At an unseen factor-of-two resolution, it scales that residual by the median of the final three adjacent-resolution RMS ratios estimated from training data only. Monotonic clipping converts the result to a valid CDF; adjacent differences are the generated 1,000-bin mass fractions. Mass-weighted \(r_{90}\) is then read from those fractions by the original bin-crossing rule, not predicted separately.

The comparator is deliberately simple: resample whole *real CLEO* members at the nearest available training resolution. Both methods receive 300 draws. Four member-wise folds hold out five complete members at every resolution; the same member labels are held out at all resolutions. Separate tests train only below 2,048 or 4,096 and compare with all 20 members at the target resolution. Because those resolutions and results had already been inspected in v1/v2, the latter tests are **retrospective**, not fresh blind validation. Development choices also used this archive, so the member-wise folds should not be treated as prospective external validation.

The comparisons are: total-variation distance (TV, percent) between the *mean* 1,000-bin mass fractions; TV between the **distributions of the exact \(r_{90}\) bins** across members; symmetric percentage error in mean \(r_{90}\); difference in mass fraction above 100 µm; and member-to-member standard deviation (SD) of \(r_{90}\). All maximum and median errors below exclude time zero. The 61 saved times are correlated, not 61 independent validation cases. The 300 draws reduce Monte Carlo noise in the emulator/baseline estimate; they do not enlarge the real 20-member validation sample.

## Results

| Test | Method | Maximum mean-DSD TV | Median \(r_{90}\)-bin distribution TV | \(r_{90}\) SD at 120 min, model versus CLEO |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,048 retrospective | DSD-first emulator | 4.68% | 10.67% | 0.640 versus 0.871 µm |
| 2,048 retrospective | Nearest real members | 2.94% | 12.67% | 0.796 versus 0.871 µm |
| 4,096 retrospective | DSD-first emulator | 3.39% | 6.33% | 0.487 versus 0.638 µm |
| 4,096 retrospective | Nearest real members | 2.60% | 5.83% | 0.814 versus 0.638 µm |

The emulator gains a little on the \(r_{90}\)-bin distribution at 2,048 but loses at 4,096. At both target resolutions it has a **larger peak mean-DSD error** than the baseline. It also underestimates the 120-minute \(r_{90}\) member spread at both targets (about 26% and 24% respectively), although the real-member baseline can overestimate the spread at 4,096. Mean \(r_{90}\) curves overlap closely in the figure; that is not a spread or full-distribution test. The highest postinitial difference in mass fraction above 100 µm is approximately 0.53 percentage points for the emulator at each target, versus 0.40 and 0.27 points for the real-member baseline.

Across the 24 within-dataset member-fold/resolution comparisons, the emulator has a lower **median** mean-DSD TV in 20, but a lower peak TV in only 14. It has a lower median exact-\(r_{90}\)-bin distribution TV in only 9 of 24, and a lower peak 100-µm tail-fraction discrepancy in only 10. Thus the modest improvement in ordinary DSD mean error does not transfer consistently to the quantities that motivated this test.

The representation itself has a measurable limit. If held-out real CDFs are projected into the training PCA and immediately reconstructed—an *oracle* check that gives the model the held-out latent scores—exact \(r_{90}\) bins are preserved at 88.0% of postinitial member-times at 2,048 and 90.5% at 4,096; at 120 minutes, the fractions are 70% and 95%. The PCA explains 98.55% and 98.18% of training CDF variance in the respective tests. This demonstrates that retaining most aggregate CDF variance does **not** guarantee preserving all threshold-bin crossings.

## Decision and scientific boundary

This is a useful *diagnostic-consistency repair*, not a calibrated generator of new CLEO ensemble members. In particular, the 300 generated draws must **not** be added to the 20 real members in the convergence bootstrap, presented as 300 new independent simulations, or used to select an 8,192/16,384 resolution. Increasing neural-network complexity on this same small archive would not supply independent validation or identify a correct high-resolution stochastic distribution. The defensible next step, if high-resolution stochastic emulation is scientifically important, is to freeze a model and validation rule first and obtain a new harmonized CLEO validation cohort under a separately approved compute plan. Otherwise, use the existing empirical resampling and retain direct CLEO evidence for convergence.
