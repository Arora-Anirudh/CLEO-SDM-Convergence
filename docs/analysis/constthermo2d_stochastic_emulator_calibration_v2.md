# Mac-only calibration follow-up for the 2-D CLEO stochastic emulator

**Status, 1 October 2026:** local exploratory analysis complete. No Levante
jobs, new CLEO simulations, or above-training-resolution synthetic cohorts were
created. This follows the first [emulator feasibility readout](constthermo2d_stochastic_emulator_exploratory_v1.md).

## What changed

The first neural model generated a complete trajectory by adding a sampled
Gaussian residual in a low-dimensional representation. Its mean was close to
withheld CLEO means, but its member distribution was too narrow. The second
model keeps the same conditional neural mean and replaces Gaussian noise with
whole-member residual trajectories resampled from the nearest training
resolution. For a genuinely unseen doubling, it scales each residual by the
median of the last three *training-only* adjacent-resolution RMS ratios.
Those ratios are approximately 0.70 for both tests. This protects the
correlation across times and diagnostics but cannot create independent new
information from the same twenty parent members.

The 2,048- and 4,096-SD-per-populated-cell outputs were inspected in the first
study. They are therefore **retrospective checks**, not fresh blind tests of a
newly selected method. The member-wise seen-resolution folds still keep whole
members out of the training set.

## Result

The modification did **not** solve the calibration problem. At withheld 2,048,
the new method's nominal 90% interval covers 72% of the held-out λ₀
member/time values, 81% of λ₆, 81% of mass-weighted terminal speed and 76% of
mass-weighted r₉₀. At withheld 4,096 the corresponding values are 86%, 81%,
81% and 75%. Time points are correlated; these percentages are descriptive,
not counts of independent validation samples. The simple unscaled
nearest-lower-resolution real-member baseline covers 89–100% on these metrics
at 2,048 and 95–100% at 4,096, although it is generally conservative.

The generated **means** remain close: at withheld 4,096 the all-postinitial
maximum mean symmetric errors are 0.25% for λ₀, 2.42% for λ₆, 1.07% for
terminal speed and 3.11% for r₉₀. Maximum 25-bin mass-DSD total variation is
0.71%. This reinforces the central distinction: reproducing a smooth
ensemble mean does not establish that synthetic members have the correct
variability. The [calibration comparison figure](../../results/analysis/constthermo2d_stochastic_emulator_calibration_v2/01_calibration_comparison.png)
displays the spread test directly.

## A diagnostic-design issue exposed by the test

The saved mass-weighted r₉₀ is **not a continuous radius measurement**. The
analysis code first forms a 1,000-bin radius histogram and returns the
geometric midpoint of the bin in which the 90% mass quantile falls
(`scripts/analyze_constthermo2d_fixedgrid_production_v1.py`,
`weighted_quantile_from_hist`). At 60 minutes all twenty 2,048-SD members have
the *exact* stored r₉₀ value 32.3385 µm; nineteen of the twenty 4,096-SD
members have that same value and the remaining member lies at 32.7656 µm.
These steps are generated by the diagnostic binning, not evidence that all
underlying continuous droplet-size distributions are identical. A model that
predicts r₉₀ as an independent smooth continuous variable can therefore
appear badly calibrated when one or two members cross a bin boundary. The
pilot's 25-bin mass-DSD representation cannot reproduce the original
1,000-bin quantile exactly.

A future model should either generate a sufficiently resolved DSD and derive
r₉₀ *using the same published diagnostic rule*, or analyse a newly defined
continuous within-bin quantile separately. It must not silently replace the
registered 1,000-bin r₉₀ used in the convergence study.

## Decision

**No calibrated generative model has been established.** In particular, do
not use either version's synthetic members to augment the fixed-20 CLEO
ensemble or to report 8,192/16,384-SD convergence. The next useful modelling
step would be a DSD-first, diagnostic-consistent model tested against a simple
empirical baseline; a larger neural network by itself is not the missing
ingredient. Any apparent improvement on the already inspected 2,048/4,096
cohorts would remain retrospective. To provide a new independent validation
point beyond 4,096, CLEO must generate genuinely new data under an explicitly
feasible and harmonized initialization protocol. The existing 8,192 cohort
uses different radius support, and that change cannot be statistically
separated from resolution with the current dataset.

## Local reproduction

From `CLEO-SDM-Convergence`, after the v1 output exists:

```bash
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/codex-sdm-mpl \
  /opt/anaconda3/bin/python \
  scripts/calibrate_constthermo2d_stochastic_emulator_v2.py --draws 300
```

The script, scores, training-only residual factors, figure and manifest are
under `results/analysis/constthermo2d_stochastic_emulator_calibration_v2/`.
All model training, plotting and inspection were done on the Mac. Levante is
not required for further modelling of these saved compact outputs. It would
be appropriate only if the scientific objective requires *new independent*
high-resolution CLEO trajectories for validation; such jobs would need a
separate design, resource estimate and explicit submission approval.
