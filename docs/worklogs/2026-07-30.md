## 4. Literature-grounded practical convergence proposal

The strict all-time/all-bin adjacent-equivalence rule was reviewed against
Shima et al. (2009), Unterstrasser et al. (2017, 2020), Dziekan and
Pawlowska (2017), Schwenkel et al. (2018), Morrison et al. (2024),
Zmijewski et al. (2024), and the current CLEO numerical-method paper.
No paper uses the project's exact unsmoothed 250/500/1000-bin L1
confidence-containment rule. Published practice instead separates mean and
variance convergence and often identifies practical convergence from a small
final change compared with much larger changes at preceding resolution
doublings.

A proposed, not-yet-canonical ADR now defines:

- a hard analytical/correctness gate;
- a one-percentage-point minimum worthwhile change, equal to 20% of the
  existing 5% analytical-error budget;
- one-sided 95% bootstrap upper bounds on the absolute change for primary
  500-bin L1, \(M_0\) bias and \(M_6\) bias;
- N/2N/4N confirmation;
- a separate ensemble-prefix stability rule;
- 250/1000-bin calculations as mandatory robustness diagnostics rather than
  automatic fine-histogram vetoes.

The existing point estimates identify 32,768 SDs as the first plausible
candidate, but this is not yet a decision because the proposed upper bounds
and all-time ensemble-prefix rule have not been computed.

The scientific audit also found a small reference-definition mismatch. The
initializer conditions an exponential-in-volume DSD on 1--75 μm, whereas the
implemented closed-form Golovin reference is untruncated. The excluded number
fractions are \(3.51\times10^{-5}\) below 1 μm and
\(3.65\times10^{-7}\) above 75 μm; the initial conditioned/untruncated mass
L1 mismatch is \(4.13\times10^{-5}\) (0.00413%). This is far too small to
explain current 0.7%--1.9% primary L1 errors, but exact-solution language must
be qualified or a support sensitivity added before publication.

## Pilot-informed extension planning

Clara gave the researcher freedom to explore provided that decisions remain
literature-grounded and defensible for a possible paper. This authorizes
method development, not an unrecorded change to the convergence criterion or
unbounded compute.

ADR 0008 therefore separates three activities:

1. use the existing 100-member outcomes to estimate per-resolution,
   per-time and per-metric variance coefficients;
2. project a fixed final member allocation before inspecting new formal
   outcomes; and
3. reserve any repeated interim stopping for a future prospectively
   alpha-adjusted or time-uniform method.

The literature supports this separation. SDM studies use ensemble sizes that
depend on the diagnostic and distinguish ensemble-mean convergence from
stochastic variance. Simulation-output literature sizes a fixed calculation
to a preassigned interval width. Cost-allocation literature motivates placing
samples where variance reduction per cost is greatest. Confidence-sequence
literature explains why ordinary fixed-sample 95% intervals cannot simply be
rechecked until one happens to pass.

The new planner:

- uses only 32,768, 65,536 and 131,072 SDs, because 16,384 is already ruled
  out by its observed late-\(M_6\) point change;
- bootstraps all primary metrics at all six decision times;
- fits the transparent planning approximation
  \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat q_n)=a/n\);
- records its discrepancy from the current percentile-bootstrap bound;
- searches both balanced and measured-cost-aware fixed allocations;
- publishes the limiting metric/time for each design; and
- sets `new_model_compute_authorized=false`.

Four focused tests prove monotone precision with member count, refusal of
unprotected optional stopping, impossibility of rescuing a point change above
the scientific margin by adding members, and a passing result from the
cost-aware allocation search. The project-owned Slurm wrapper is
non-overwriting and checksum-validates both `analysis_v4` and `practical_v2`.
It requests one serial CPU, 940 MiB and 20 minutes on `shared`, contains no
`srun`, and launches no CLEO executable.
